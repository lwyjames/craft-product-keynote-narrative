# Timing and delivery

## Calibrate from an actual read

Use the closest relevant sample of the user's own spoken Chinese and its measured duration. Count visible Chinese characters and spoken English words consistently in the sample and candidate draft; note how numerals, product names, and acronyms are spoken. Estimate speech time by `draft spoken units ÷ sample spoken units × sample speech seconds`. If the measured sample includes pauses or demo time, separate them if possible. If separation is impossible, explain that the calibrated rate includes those pauses.

Calculate the total page time as **speech + intentional pauses + demo/transition time**. A slide that plays a long animation may need fewer spoken words, not more. State the assumed seconds for each component; do not call a written estimate a measured rehearsal.

If no measured rate exists, write for the requested time with conservative room for breathing and demo. Label time provisional and invite a later read-time measurement. Do not impose one universal words-per-minute or reuse a rate from a different speaker.

If an earlier note overshoots its target, recalculate with the measured duration. Remove duplicate setup, extra examples, and technical detours before cutting the line that makes the value intelligible. Recheck the new estimate; allow a small margin below the maximum target when the stage timing is strict.

## Spoken Chinese review

Read the note aloud or mentally parse it in breaths. Prefer short clauses with clear referents. Explain what the audience is looking at when the visual changes. Put the payoff near the hero moment, then make space for the audience to see it. Avoid English product terminology or technical acronyms when natural Chinese is clearer, except approved product names.

The user's historical targets, such as 40–50 seconds for one demonstration and 60–70 seconds for another, were assignment-specific. Never silently turn them into universal defaults.
